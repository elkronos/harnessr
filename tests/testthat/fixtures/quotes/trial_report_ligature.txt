Eﬃcacy and safety of a once-weekly inhaled corticosteroid in adults with moderate asthma

Methods

We conducted a multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 14 sites in three countries. Adults aged 18 to 65 years with a conﬁrmed diagnosis of asthma and at least two exacerbations in the previous year were eligible. Adults with a body mass index of ≥ 40 or a smoking history of more than 10 pack-years were excluded. Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the inhaled corticosteroid or to a matching placebo. The ﬁrst dose was given in the clinic oﬃce under supervision, and later doses were taken at home.

The primary outcome was the rate of severe exacerbations over 52 weeks. Secondary outcomes were the change in forced expiratory volume in one second, peak expiratory ﬂow, and the score on the Asthma Control Questionnaire. Suﬃcient power was deﬁned as 90 percent to detect a 30 percent reduction in the exacerbation rate.

Results

A total of 412 participants were randomised, of whom 206 received the active drug and 206 received placebo. The mean age was 44.7 years and 58 percent of participants were women. Baseline lung function was similar in the two groups, with a mean forced expiratory volume of 2.31 litres.

The rate of severe exacerbations was 0.42 per participant-year in the active group and 0.71 in the placebo group, a rate ratio of 0.59. The diﬀerence was statistically signiﬁcant and was consistent across the prespeciﬁed subgroups. Peak expiratory ﬂow improved by 21 litres per minute more with the active drug than with placebo. The proportion of participants with at least one severe exacerbation was lower with the active drug (p = 0.003), and rescue inhaler use fell in both groups (p < 0.001).

The eﬀect on the Asthma Control Questionnaire did not reach the minimal clinically important diﬀerence of 0.5 points. Participants in the active group reported fewer nights aﬀected by symptoms, but the diﬀerence was small and diﬃcult to interpret.

Safety

Adverse events were reported by 61 percent of participants in the active group and 57 percent in the placebo group. Oral candidiasis occurred in 9 participants receiving the active drug and in 2 participants receiving placebo. No deaths occurred during the trial, and no participant was withdrawn because of a serious adverse event.

Fluid retention was not observed in either group. Three participants in the active group had a transient rise in blood glucose, all of whom had diabetes at baseline. The oﬃcial safety review found no speciﬁc signal that would aﬀect the beneﬁt-risk proﬁle.

Discussion

The once-weekly regimen reduced severe exacerbations by about 41 percent without a clear eﬀect on day-to-day symptom control. The trial was not designed to compare the drug with daily inhaled corticosteroids, and the ﬁndings should not be extended to children. Financial support came from a public research council, which had no role in the analysis.

Adherence was measured with electronic counters ﬁtted to each inhaler. Mean adherence was 93 percent in both groups, so the diﬀerence in eﬀect is unlikely to reﬂect diﬀerences in use. Staﬀ at each site were trained to check inhaler technique at every visit.
