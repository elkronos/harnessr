Efficacy and safety of a once-weekly inhaled corticosteroid
in adults with moderate asthma

Methods

We conducted a multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled
trial at 14 sites in three countries. Adults aged 18 to 65
years with a confirmed diagnosis of asthma and at least two exacer-
bations in the previous year were eligible. Adults with a
body mass index of ≥ 40 or a smoking history of more than 10
pack-years were excluded. Participants were randomly assi-
gned in a 1:1 ratio to the inhaled corticosteroid or to a matc-
hing placebo. The first dose was given in the clinic office
under supervision, and later doses were taken at home.

The primary outcome was the rate of severe exacerbations
over 52 weeks. Secondary outcomes were the change in forced expir-
atory volume in one second, peak expiratory flow, and the
score on the Asthma Control Questionnaire. Sufficient power
was defined as 90 percent to detect a 30 percent reduction
in the exacerbation rate.

Results

A total of 412 participants were randomised, of whom 206 rece-
ived the active drug and 206 received placebo. The mean age
was 44.7 years and 58 percent of participants were women. Base-
line lung function was similar in the two groups, with a
mean forced expiratory volume of 2.31 litres.

The rate of severe exacerbations was 0.42 per
participant-year in the active group and 0.71 in the placebo
group, a rate ratio of 0.59. The difference was statis-
tically significant and was consistent across the prespe-
cified subgroups. Peak expiratory flow improved by 21 litres
per minute more with the active drug than with placebo. The propo-
rtion of participants with at least one severe exacerbation
was lower with the active drug (p = 0.003), and rescue inh-
aler use fell in both groups (p < 0.001).

The effect on the Asthma Control Questionnaire did not reach
the minimal clinically important difference of 0.5 points. Partic-
ipants in the active group reported fewer nights affected by
symptoms, but the difference was small and difficult to
interpret.

Safety

Adverse events were reported by 61 percent of participants
in the active group and 57 percent in the placebo group.
Oral candidiasis occurred in 9 participants receiving the
active drug and in 2 participants receiving placebo. No
deaths occurred during the trial, and no participant was with-
drawn because of a serious adverse event.

Fluid retention was not observed in either group. Three partic-
ipants in the active group had a transient rise in blood
glucose, all of whom had diabetes at baseline. The official
safety review found no specific signal that would affect the
benefit-risk profile.

Discussion

The once-weekly regimen reduced severe exacerbations by
about 41 percent without a clear effect on day-to-day sym-
ptom control. The trial was not designed to compare the drug
with daily inhaled corticosteroids, and the findings should
not be extended to children. Financial support came from a
public research council, which had no role in the analysis.

Adherence was measured with electronic counters fitted to
each inhaler. Mean adherence was 93 percent in both groups,
so the difference in effect is unlikely to reflect diffe-
rences in use. Staff at each site were trained to check inh-
aler technique at every visit.
